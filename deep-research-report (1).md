## What Is a Loan Against LIC Policy?

A **loan against an LIC policy** lets you borrow money using your life insurance policy as collateral. It’s a **secured loan** – you pledge your policy’s surrender value instead of liquidating it. I find this option great for emergency funds, because **your policy stays active** while you get cash. For example, if you have a traditional endowment policy that’s paid for 2–3 years, you can typically borrow around **80% of its surrender value**【22†L13-L16】, and still keep the insurance cover in force. 

Because it’s secured by the policy, **interest rates are much lower** than unsecured loans. LIC usually charges about **9%–10% per year**【24†L1-L4】【23†L1-L4】 (compare that to 12%+ on personal loans).

## Eligibility for LIC Policy Loan

Before you apply, let’s ensure you **qualify for the loan**. Here’s the quick checklist:

- **Policy Type:** Must be a **traditional life policy** (endowment, whole life, money-back, Jeevan Anand, etc.). Term or pure ULIP plans **do not** qualify.  
- **Premiums Paid:** You should have paid premiums for at least **2–3 years** (policy paid-up). In most plans, the minimum is **two full years** of premiums.  
- **Policy Status:** The policy must be **active/in-force** (not surrendered or fully paid-up long ago).  
- **Surrender Value:** The policy must have some surrender value built up. No surrender value means no loan.  
- **Policy Assignment:** You’ll assign (pledge) the policy to LIC/lender during the loan, but the insurance cover continues.

In short, if you’re the policyholder of an active LIC endowment plan with >2 years of premiums paid, **you can likely get a loan**. (We’ll cover common **disqualifiers** in FAQs below.)

## How to Calculate Your LIC Policy Loan

The loan amount is calculated as a percentage of your policy’s **surrender value**. As a rule of thumb:

- **Active (in-force) policy:** You can take up to **90%** of the surrender value.  
- **Paid-up policy:** You can take up to **80%**.  

*(LIC may vary percentages for certain plans, but the calculator will use 90%/80% by default.)*

**Example Calculation:** Suppose your policy’s surrender value is **₹2,00,000** after 5 years. If it’s in-force, you can get up to 90%, i.e. **₹1,80,000** loan. If you prefer a conservative approach, you might borrow ₹1,70,000 (85%). At **10% interest**, the annual interest is ₹17,000. If you choose to pay interest quarterly or monthly, your EMI would be much lower than a typical personal loan. (You can see exact EMI amounts in the calculator output.)

Using our **LIC Loan Calculator** above, you simply enter 2,00,000 as surrender value. It instantly does this calculation for you and shows the results.

## Required Documents & Application Process

Getting a loan is easy if you have the **right documents**. You’ll need:

- **Original Policy Bond:** The actual LIC policy document.  
- **Identity Proof:** (Aadhar, PAN, passport, etc.)  
- **Address Proof:** (Utility bill, Aadhar, etc.)  
- **Cancelled Cheque:** For the bank account where funds will go.  
- **Loan Application Form:** You’ll fill this out (online or at a branch).  
- **Assignment Deed:** LIC will have you sign a form assigning the policy to them while the loan is outstanding.

### How to Apply Online

I recommend trying the **online route** first – it’s quick and paperless. Follow these steps (based on LIC’s portal):

1. **Register/Login:** Go to [LIC Customer Portal](https://www.licindia.in/). Sign in or create an account.  
2. **Online Loans:** Navigate to *Online Services → Policy Loans*.  
3. **Request Loan:** Enter your policy number and surrender value. Fill out the loan amount you want (up to the eligible limit).  
4. **Upload Documents:** You may upload scanned ID/address proofs as requested.  
5. **Submit:** Submit the online application. LIC will process it (usually within 1–2 business days). You’ll get confirmation and funds disbursed to your bank.  

*(Image suggestion: [Embed a flowchart or screenshot of LIC’s online portal steps]*)

### How to Apply Offline

If you prefer going in person:

1. Visit your **nearest LIC branch** (or appoint an LIC agent).  
2. **Fill Loan Form:** Get and fill the “Loan Against Policy” form.  
3. **Submit Documents:** Give the policy bond, KYC docs, cancelled cheque.  
4. **Assign the Policy:** Sign the agreement assigning the policy to LIC/lender.  
5. **Disbursal:** LIC will sanction the loan and deposit money to your account (often within 2–3 days).

Either way, the process is straightforward. The key is to **assign your policy as collateral**; otherwise, no loan is possible.

## Repayment Options

One of the great things about LIC policy loans is **flexible repayment**:

- **Interest-Only:** You can opt to pay just the interest (quarterly or half-yearly) and keep the policy intact. The principal gets deducted only at maturity.  
- **EMI (Interest + Principal):** Or choose to pay fixed EMIs covering principal + interest, which shortens the debt tenure.

**Example:** On a ₹1,00,000 loan at 10% for 5 years, you could:
- Pay interest only (~₹10,000/year) and then clear ₹1,00,000 at the end.  
- Or pay EMI of ~₹2,124/month (for 60 months) to zero the loan.

The calculator shows both options. If you fail to pay interest, LIC will deduct it from your policy’s eventual maturity/surrender payout.

## Benefits of Loan Against LIC Policy

I encourage folks to consider these advantages before applying:

- **Lower Interest:** Usually ~9-10%【24†L1-L4】 vs 12-18% on other personal loans【23†L1-L4】.  
- **No Credit Check:** Since it’s secured by your policy, even if your credit score is low, you can get a loan.  
- **Quick Disbursal:** LIC loans often process within days, much faster than bank loans.  
- **No Impact on Cover:** Your life insurance cover stays active during the loan.  
- **Flexible Repayment:** Pay interest only or choose EMIs; LIC doesn’t penalize prepayment.  
- **Tax-Friendly:** Proceeds are tax-free (loans aren’t treated as income).

## Disadvantages / Risks to Consider

However, be clear on the downsides:

- **Interest Compounding:** If you don’t pay interest, it accrues (reducing maturity/claim).  
- **Reduced Maturity Benefit:** Outstanding loan + interest will be deducted from your final payout if you don’t repay fully.  
- **Eligibility Limits:** Term insurance, ULIPs, annuities usually **don’t qualify**. (We’ll cover this in FAQs.)  
- **Policy Pledge:** During the loan, LIC holds your policy (you won’t get bonuses or surrender rights beyond the pledged amount).  

Basically, treat it like any loan: Borrow what you need and pay it back as quickly as possible.

## LIC Loan vs Other Loans

**LIC Loan vs Personal Loan:** LIC policy loans offer **lower rates (9-10%)**【23†L1-L4】, but a personal loan might be easier to get without collateral. Personal loans have higher interest (12-20%) and rigid EMIs, but you keep your policy intact. If you have a strong credit history, compare offers.

**LIC Loan vs Surrender:** If you’re tempted to cancel your policy for cash, consider a loan first. Surrendering often gives only a partial payout and ends your coverage. A loan lets you **keep your insurance benefits** while still getting funds【2†L117-L124】.
