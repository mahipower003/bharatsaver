## What is LIC? (Overview of LIC Policy)

LIC (Life Insurance Corporation of India) is a **state-owned life insurance company** (est. 1956) offering various endowment and insurance plans【30†L385-L392】. An LIC plan combines **life cover** with a **savings component**. You pay annual premiums; on policy maturity (or death), LIC pays out the **sum assured plus accumulated bonuses**. Importantly, LIC is backed by a **sovereign guarantee** under the LIC Act, Sec. 37【4†L49-L55】. That means the *core sum assured* you choose is **secure and guaranteed** by the government. However, because part of your premium pays for insurance and expenses, the *effective returns on LIC endowment plans* are typically in the **4–6% range**【26†L1-L4】 (much lower than market-linked plans). 

- **Premiums & Coverage:** You commit to paying a fixed premium (e.g. ₹50,000/year) for a term (often 15–25 years). You get life cover; if anything happens, dependents get at least the sum assured plus bonuses.  
- **Bonuses (Returns):** Each year LIC declares a “reversionary bonus” (₹ per ₹1000 SA) and sometimes a one-time Final Additional Bonus (FAB) at maturity. These are added to your payout【4†L76-L84】【4†L106-L114】. For example, a ₹10 lakh sum assured, 20-year term, with a ₹40 bonus rate, yields: **Maturity = ₹10L + ₹8L bonus + FAB (e.g. ₹0.8L) = ₹18.8L**【4†L106-L114】.  
- **Guaranteed Payout:** The guaranteed part of LIC payout is the **sum assured** (your chosen coverage). Bonuses, while historically given, are not guaranteed but fairly stable (LIC’s actuary sets them annually).  
- **Tax Benefits (LIC):** LIC premiums are tax-deductible under Section 80C (up to ₹1.5L/year). The maturity proceeds and death benefits are tax-free under Section 10(10D) *if* the premium is ≤ 10% of sum assured【14†L334-L340】. If your premium >10% of SA, the maturity amount’s income portion can become taxable. (Also, any death benefit paid to heirs is tax-exempt.【28†L7-L10】)  
- **Liquidity:** LIC policies lock in your funds for at least 2–3 years. Early surrender is possible, but the value you get back (Guaranteed Surrender Value) is much less than premiums paid. You can loan against your policy after a few years (up to ~90% of the surrender value). If you stop paying premiums (after 2–3 years), the policy can become “paid-up” with reduced SA.  
- **Suitability:** LIC is best when **insurance cover is a priority**. If you want guaranteed low-risk savings plus life cover, LIC policies (like traditional endowments or money-back plans) fit. But for pure savings growth, LIC yields are modest.

## What is PPF? (Overview of PPF)

The **Public Provident Fund (PPF)** is a long-term savings scheme launched by the Government in 1968【14†L371-L379】. It’s **100% government-backed**, so your deposits earn a fixed interest rate (currently **7.1% p.a.**【14†L374-L379】【25†L248-L254】, set by the Finance Ministry each quarter). PPF is *not* an insurance or mutual fund – it’s a social security saving vehicle with tax benefits.

- **Investment & Returns:** You contribute up to ₹1.5 lakh per year (min ₹500/year) for 15 years. Each deposit and its accrued interest (compounded annually) forms your corpus. Because it’s government-guaranteed, PPF yields are fixed; at 7.1% p.a. your investment doubles roughly every 10 years. This compounding makes PPF a powerful wealth-builder over the long term.  
- **Interest Rate:** The PPF rate is reviewed every quarter. It has remained at 7.1% since 2020【25†L248-L254】. For example, investing ₹50,000/year into PPF for 20 years yields about **₹20.7 lakh**【26†L1-L4】【30†L394-L402】 (vs ~₹18L in LIC at 5–6%).  
- **Tax Benefits (PPF):** PPF is “EEE”: Exempt-Taxable-Free. That means: your contributions (up to ₹1.5L) are deductible under 80C; the interest is entirely tax-free; and the final maturity amount is also tax-free【14†L377-L379】【28†L1-L4】. In short, you pay tax on nothing in a PPF investment.  
- **Lock-in & Liquidity:** PPF has a 15-year lock-in (though the account can be extended in 5-year blocks indefinitely). Partial withdrawals are allowed from the 7th year on (with limits), and loans can be taken from the 3rd to 6th year. Overall, PPF is *less flexible* than a regular FD but more liquid than a locked LIC plan.  
- **Suitability:** PPF is ideal for **long-term goal savings** like retirement or child’s future education. Its guaranteed ~7% returns and tax-free status beat most safe options. Since there’s no life cover, it’s purely for wealth accumulation.

Below is a concise comparison of key features and differences【30†L394-L402】【14†L374-L379】:

| Parameter | LIC (Traditional Endowment) | PPF |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Nature** | **Insurance + Investment:** Combines life cover with savings【30†L385-L392】. | **Pure Saving Scheme:** Government-backed long-term savings. |
| **Return Type** | Bonuses (simple reversionary + FAB). Typically ~4–6% effective (after all costs)【26†L1-L4】. | **Fixed Interest:** Currently ~7.1% p.a.【14†L374-L379】【25†L248-L254】 (subject to quarterly govt. review). |
| **Tenure/Lock-in** | Varies (10–30 yrs). Usually at least 5 years premium paid before surrender allowed. | **15-year lock-in** (then can extend)【30†L397-L402】. Partial withdrawals allowed from year 7. |
| **Tax Benefits** | Premiums qualify under 80C. Maturity/death proceeds tax-free under Sec 10(10D) *if* premium ≤10% of SA (u/s 80C)【28†L7-L10】【30†L399-L402】. | Contributions u/s 80C. **Interest and maturity are completely tax-free** (EEE)【14†L377-L379】. |
| **Minimum/Maximum Investment** | Premium fixed by plan (depends on SA, term). No government limit. | Min ₹500/year, Max ₹1.5 lakh/year. |
| **Risk** | Low (guaranteed SA). Traditional plans: no market risk. ULIPs (market-linked) have higher risk. | Very Low. Returns guaranteed by government. Zero credit risk. |

| **Purpose** | Financial protection for family (life cover) + disciplined savings. | Long-term capital growth, especially for goals (retirement, education) with tax-free interest. |
| **Maturity Benefit** | Sum Assured + bonuses (tax-free if conditions met). Paid at policy end or on death earlier. | Accumulated corpus of deposits + interest (tax-free). Paid at maturity or on account closure. |

This table highlights why **PPF typically gives higher post-tax returns**. PPF’s interest is more than LIC’s bonus rate, and PPF’s funds are fully yours (with tax exemption). LIC, however, offers life cover and sometimes more flexibility (e.g., loan against policy) that PPF doesn’t.

## LIC vs PPF: Pros & Cons

### Advantages of LIC Endowment Plans:
- **Life Cover:** Your family gets the assured sum and bonuses on your death. (PPF has no life cover.)  
- **Disciplined Savings:** Commitment to premiums forces saving.  
- **Guaranteed Payout:** SA is guaranteed (plus *expected* bonuses).  
- **Loan Option:** You can borrow against policy.  
- **Tax Savings:** Premiums reduce your taxable income (u/s 80C), and maturity/death benefits are tax-free if conditions met.  

### Disadvantages of LIC Plans:
- **Lower Returns:** Real returns ~4–6%【26†L1-L4】, generally below inflation.  
- **Complexity & Fees:** Part of premium covers mortality cost and agent commissions, reducing savings component.  
- **Illiquidity:** Surrendering early yields very low value. Paid-up reduces your benefits if you stop premiums.  
- **Tax Caution:** If premium >10% of SA, maturity gains lose tax-exemption【28†L7-L10】.  
- **Market Conditions:** No opportunity to earn more when markets do well (unlike equities).

### Advantages of PPF:
- **High Guaranteed Returns:** Consistently around 7% (currently 7.1%【14†L374-L379】), beating most fixed schemes.  
- **Tax-Free Growth:** Deposits (80C), interest, and maturity all tax-free【14†L377-L379】, making effective yield very attractive.  
- **Safety:** Backed by government; absolutely no risk of capital loss.  
- **Good for Goals:** Ideal for retirement, children’s education, or property down-payment as a forced savings.  
- **Loan Facility:** After 3 yrs, you can take loans (2 loans max) against the balance. Partial withdrawals also allowed after 7 yrs.

### Disadvantages of PPF:
- **Long Lock-in:** 15-year maturity (though extendable). Early closure incurs penalty (if before 15 yrs, only under specific conditions).  
- **Contribution Limit:** Max ₹1.5L/year. If you want to invest more, you’d need other avenues.  
- **No Insurance:** Does not provide any life cover or family security.  
- **Interest Rate Changes:** Though stable, the rate can be cut or raised by the government (usually downward pressure if inflation is low).  

## Who Should Choose Which? (Goal-Based Advice)

- **If You Need Life Insurance + Savings:** LIC policies (like endowment, money-back, ULIPs) are tailored for those who want protection plus savings. E.g., young parents might buy LIC to ensure children’s future even if the parent dies.  
- **If You Want Safe, Tax-Free Growth:** PPF is better if your priority is **maximizing corpus** with no risk. For goals like retirement or a child’s education fund, PPF often outperforms LIC. For example, a 20-year PPF investment at 7.1% can yield **5–6 lakhs more** than the same LIC premium schedule (as seen above).  
- **Retirement Planning:** PPF’s lock-in and tax-free compounding make it ideal for retirement (especially since [policybazaar notes](#) PPF is “particularly suited for retirement planning”【30†L434-L442】). You could also balance with safe equity (through mutual funds) for higher growth, but between LIC and PPF alone, PPF wins for retirement corpus.  
- **Child Education/Marriage:** You can use both. PPF’s steady growth is excellent for a *pure savings fund*. However, if you want to combine it with cover, LIC has child plans (like “Jeevan Anand” or “Bachatbima”) which save plus insure. Just be mindful LIC’s effective return will be lower. A balanced approach: lock ₹1.5L in PPF every year (max it out) and if you want life cover, take a separate term plan or LIC term assurance.  
- **Debt vs Equity:** Remember, LIC is not a substitute for equity. If your goal is *aggressive wealth creation*, look at mutual funds (SIPs) instead (they often beat both LIC and PPF but carry market risk). Use [FD vs PPF calculator] or [Mutual Fund screener] (see below) to explore alternatives.

## Calculator Logic (How We Compare LIC and PPF Returns)

Our **LIC vs PPF calculator** uses these formulas:

- **PPF Maturity:** Each year’s deposit grows at fixed interest. Mathematically, for annual deposit P, rate r, and n years:  
  \[
  \text{PPF\_Maturity} = P \times \frac{(1+r)^n - 1}{r}  
  \]  
  (Here r = 0.071 for 7.1%.)

- **LIC Maturity (Endowment):** For an LIC endowment with *Sum Assured (SA)*, *Term (n)*, *Bonus rate (b)* per ₹1000 SA, and possibly a *Final Additional Bonus (FAB)* per ₹1000 SA:  
  \[
  \text{LIC\_Maturity} = \text{SA} + \bigl(\frac{\text{SA}}{1000} \times b \times n \bigr) + \bigl(\frac{\text{SA}}{1000} \times \text{FAB}\bigr).
  \]  
  This assumes a constant declared bonus **b** each year (for example, ₹40 per ₹1000 SA). In practice, LIC guarantees that **SA** is returned, and bonuses accumulate annually【4†L76-L84】.  
  - If you want to compare an “effective investment” of ₹50k/year: choose SA such that the annual premium ≈ ₹50k (LIC’s premium depends on age, plan). The calculator can invert a given premium to find SA using LIC premium rates (or you can adjust SA manually).

## FAQ (Featured Snippet Style)

**Q1: Which is better, LIC or PPF?**  
**A:** For pure returns, PPF is better. It offers ~7% guaranteed returns (7.1% currently)【30†L394-L402】【25†L248-L254】, compounded tax-free, making it superior to LIC’s typical 4–6%. But LIC gives life insurance cover too. If your goal is wealth accumulation, PPF usually wins; if you need insurance + savings, LIC may suit.

**Q2: What are the tax benefits of PPF vs LIC?**  
**A:** Both fall under Section 80C. LIC premiums (for endowment policies) are deductible under 80C, and if conditions are met, maturity/death proceeds are tax-free under 10(10D)【30†L399-L402】【28†L7-L10】. PPF contributions (up to ₹1.5L) are also 80C-deductible; importantly **PPF interest and final corpus are fully tax-free** (EEE status)【14†L377-L379】.

**Q3: What returns can I expect?**  
**A:** PPF has a fixed interest rate (currently 7.1%)【14†L374-L379】. LIC returns vary by plan, but traditional endowment plans have historically given around 4–6% effective post-bonus【26†L1-L4】. For example, ₹50k/yr for 20 yrs yields about ₹20–21L in PPF vs ~₹17–18L in a standard LIC plan.

**Q4: Which is better for retirement?**  
**A:** Typically **PPF**. It locks in money for the long term, provides guaranteed growth (7%+ p.a.), and tax-free corpus – ideal for building a retirement fund【30†L434-L442】. LIC policies give some savings plus cover, but their low returns mean they’re less effective for pure retirement savings unless you also need life cover.

**Q5: Which is better for my child’s education fund?**  
**A:** PPF again gives higher tax-free returns for long-term goals like education. However, if you want to ensure the child is covered by insurance until maturity, you could consider LIC’s child plans (e.g. "Jeevan Anand – Child Plan") along with PPF. A combined approach (max out PPF + a separate term plan) is often recommended.

**Q6: How does liquidity compare?**  
**A:** PPF funds are locked for 15 years (with partial withdrawal allowed after year 7). LIC plans typically require you to pay at least 5 years of premium; you can surrender, but the payout is usually much lower than premiums paid in early years【11†L42-L50】. LIC policies allow loans against the policy (up to 90% of surrender value) which can provide liquidity without killing the policy【11†L49-L56】.

**Q7: Can I invest in both LIC and PPF?**  
**A:** Yes. They serve different purposes. Many people invest in PPF for the best post-tax returns and still buy a pure term insurance for life cover. Buying an LIC plan solely as an investment is often not optimal. A balanced strategy is: use PPF for savings, and take term insurance (which is cheaper) or a small LIC plan for cover.

**Q8: What is the interest rate on PPF vs LIC?**  
**A:** PPF’s official interest is about **7.1% p.a.** (as of 2025)【25†L248-L254】. LIC traditional plans don’t have a single “rate”; the bonus rate (e.g., ₹40 per ₹1000 SA per year) effectively yields 4–6% on the total premiums. ULIPs (market-linked) can fluctuate based on markets (not covered here).

**Q9: Which one has a better lock-in and withdrawal facility?**  
**A:** PPF lock-in is longer (15 years) but you can withdraw partially (from year 7) or extend the account. LIC requires a 5-year premium lock-in; surrender earlier yields guaranteed surrender value. If you stop premiums after 2–3 years, LIC policy goes paid-up, reducing benefits. PPF offers more flexibility through loans and partial withdrawals (though only after a few years).

Each of these Q&A addresses a common search query like "LIC vs PPF which is better", "ppf vs lic tax benefits", "should I invest in lic or ppf", etc., aiming for featured snippets.

## Conclusion

In summary, **PPF is generally the better investment if you want safe, tax-free growth**【14†L374-L379】【30†L394-L402】. It beats LIC on returns and liquidity (after initial lock-in). **LIC should be chosen if insurance cover is a priority**, or as a part of a diversified plan. For most investors, a smart approach is: **max out PPF (or NPS) for savings and returns, and separately buy a term life insurance (or small LIC) for protection**.
